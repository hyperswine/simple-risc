# fprisc-84: the RV32IM canon at 84 MHz

Kind: preset and board qualification, 2026-11-08. Follows
[IM-CANON](2026-11-08-IM-CANON.md), whose clock sweep found 84 MHz to be the
highest CPU clock the IM core closes at every placement option.

## Result

`fprisc-84` is qualified: built from the committed tree it closes 84 MHz at
86.021 MHz, +0.280 ns (the sweep's figure to the last digit), and on the
board it passes the processor, RV32M (30,720) and RV32I (38,400) checks at
84 MHz and the FP-RISC suite 7/7. Build id `28ed47fb2abb9afc`.

| Canon | Clock | Timing | Classifier 512/128, per run | Against IMC at 81 |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| IMC (`dcd220d`, 6 November) | 81 | 84.447 MHz, +0.504 ns, 2 of 4 placements | 6.799 s | - |
| IM (`3b4052d`) | 81 | 88.149 MHz, +1.001 ns, 4 of 4 | 7.278 s | +7.0% |
| **IM, this page** | **84** | **86.021 MHz, +0.280 ns, 4 of 4** | **7.128 s** | **+4.8%** |

The 3.7% of clock buys 2.1% of time: the SDRAM side stays at 48 MHz, so
the cache-miss share of the classifier does not scale, as every clock step
in this record has shown. The remaining 4.8% against the IMC canon is the
instruction-fetch cost of RV32IM; the ways to recover it are in
[IM-CANON](2026-11-08-IM-CANON.md)'s next steps (the slow-path area for a
robust 87, and later a narrower RVC front end).

Both IM images are the same 94,324 bytes from the same compiler; they differ
only in the baked-in clock.

## The preset

`fprisc-84` is `fprisc-81` with the CPU clock at 84 MHz and the placer target
at 92: RV32IM core, two-way 64 KiB cache, SDRAM 48 MHz behind the bridge,
placement 3, the `fprisc-81.plan` floorplan, map 3, route 1, route fan-out 8.
84 = 27 x 28 / 9 is a legal PLL setting (`build.sh` lists it). FP-RISC's
preset table and runner know it (fprisc `0e13f68`), with `FPR_RVC=0` and a
runtime clock of 84 MHz.

In the sweep that chose it the IM netlist closed 84 MHz at placements 0, 1,
3 and 4 with +0.32, +0.31, +0.28 and +0.22 ns; 87 and 90 closed two of four
and 93 one. The preset build's own result is in the table above.

The suite ran from a worktree of the 6 November compiler because today's
`fprisc` head cannot build rv32 bare-metal images; the preset and runner
changes are committed on `fprisc` main and cherry-picked into that worktree.

The board holds the `fprisc-84` image in SRAM (flash unchanged); the
`fprisc-81` IM image and the IMC image (`output/tangnano20k/fprisc-81-imc-7823d65e/`)
are both still available to load.
