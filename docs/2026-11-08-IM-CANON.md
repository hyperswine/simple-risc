# The RV32IM core becomes the canon

Kind: preset change, board qualification and the first clock sweep,
2026-11-08. Follows [IM-ONLY-BUILD](2026-11-08-IM-ONLY-BUILD.md), which
measured the core without compressed instructions; this page makes it the
`fprisc-81` preset and qualifies it on the board.

## Result

`fprisc-81` is now the RV32IM core: `SDRAM_RVC=0`, placement 3, the
`fprisc-81.plan` floorplan, CPU 81 / SDRAM 48 MHz as before. `fprisc-48` and
`fprisc-72` stay RV32IMC. FP-RISC's preset sets `FPR_RVC=0` for `fprisc-81`,
and its runner and classifier check follow the preset instead of assuming
compressed code.

| | IMC canon (`dcd220d`, 6 November) | IM canon (this page) |
|---|---|---|
| Timing, placement 3 | 84.447 MHz, +0.504 ns | **88.149 MHz, +1.001 ns** |
| Placements closing 81 MHz | 2 of 4 distinct | **4 of 4** |
| Occupied slices | 8,717 (84%) | **8,053 (78%)** |
| Whole-design logic | 15,074 | 12,684 |
| Board checks at 81 MHz (processor, 30,720 RV32M, 38,400 RV32I) | pass | **pass** |
| FP-RISC suite | 7/7 | **7/7** |
| Classifier 512/128, per run | 6.799 s (74,932-byte image) | **7.278 s (94,324-byte image)** |

So the canon is now robust to placement, 15.9% smaller in logic, and **7.0%
slower on the classifier at the same clock**, exactly what the simulation
priced (+7.5% cycles at 81/48). The two classifier images come from the same
compiler (`fprisc` 9451dca, with the preset change applied), so the
comparison is the instruction set and nothing else.

The preset built from source reproduces the experiment's 88.149 MHz to the
last digit although its ROM carries a different build id; the IMC canon's
placement moved on far smaller perturbations. Build id `f0a55aba3384faf4`,
ISA `rv32im`.

## What changed

- `preset.sh`: `fprisc-81` sets `SDRAM_RVC=0`; the other presets keep
  `SDRAM_RVC=1` and refuse the override.
- `preset_manifest.py`: `CONFIG_81.compressed = False`, so the ROM identity
  reports `rv32im` and the FP-RISC runner, which compares its own copy of the
  configuration, refuses an IMC manifest.
- FP-RISC `machine/tangnano20k/preset.sh`, `tools/run_tangnano20k_examples.py`
  and `tests/check_tangnano20k_dtree.py` (fprisc `5a6140c`): `FPR_RVC` per
  preset, and the classifier check builds the ISA its caller asks for and
  reports it.

The core's source is unchanged: the IM front end has been in the design all
along (`RISCV_COMPRESSED` undefined) and in CI's generated-RTL regression,
which runs every check on both cores.

The previous IMC image is kept at `output/tangnano20k/fprisc-81-imc-7823d65e/`
and can be loaded again with `openFPGALoader`; its source is `dcd220d` with
`SDRAM_RVC=1`.

## Clock sweep

With margin at 81 MHz the question is how much of the 7% comes back from
the clock. The IM netlist was rebuilt at four higher CPU clocks (the
SDRAM stays at 48 MHz behind the bridge; the placer target is the clock
plus about 10%) and routed at placements 0, 1, 3 and 4 (2 duplicates 1):

| CPU clock | Placement 0 | Placement 1 (= 2) | Placement 3 | Placement 4 | Pass |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 81 | 84.41, +0.50 | 85.24, +0.61 | 88.15, +1.00 | 82.19, +0.18 | 4 of 4 |
| 84 | 86.29, +0.32 | 86.28, +0.31 | 86.02, +0.28 | 85.59, +0.22 | **4 of 4** |
| 87 | 86.71, -0.04 | 88.30, +0.17 | 87.78, +0.10 | 86.98, -0.003 | 2 of 4 |
| 90 | 89.46, -0.07 | 90.07, +0.01 | 90.44, +0.05 | 87.30, -0.34 | 2 of 4 |
| 93 | 88.55, -0.54 | 89.69, -0.40 | 93.00, 0.00 | 92.61, -0.05 | 1 of 4 |

(MHz and worst setup slack in ns; occupied slices 8,036 to 8,132 throughout.)

The placer lands within 0.2 ns of whatever it is asked for when it can, so
the margins shrink as the target rises; what the table shows is which
clocks every placement still closes. **84 MHz is the highest clock every placement closes**; 87 and 90 are
two-of-four lotteries, which is where the IMC core sat at 81.

## What can be done next

In order of what the data supports:

1. **Decide the clock.** 84 MHz recovers 3.7 of the 7 points at the same
   robustness as 81 (every placement, margins +0.22 to +0.32 ns); 87 would
   recover all of it on the two placements that close, which is the IMC
   canon's robustness and the thing this change was meant to end. The
   recommendation is 84 as the canon, qualified the same way (a preset,
   the board checks, the suite, the classifier), and 87 or 90 only when
   the area work below has restored the margin. Both 84 and 87 are legal
   PLL settings.
2. **Use the room.** With 664 slices free and every placement closing,
   the IPC changes that would not place on the IMC core can be re-priced
   under the revised method: the load-use forwarding L1 (1.010x), E1-to-D
   hazard depth, and the front-end work from the IPC plan. Each now gets a
   distribution rather than a draw.
3. **Shrink the slow path anyway.** It is 37% of a smaller core: the divide
   unit (807 LUTs), the bus response path and the CSR muxes are the same
   targets as before, and the aggregate branch's DSP counters (-454) apply
   unchanged. They buy further margin or a higher clock.
4. **Give RVC a second chance later, narrower.** The 7% is instruction
   fetch (2.6x the code fills). A front end that expands compressed pairs in
   the fetch stage without the assembly stage and F queue would recover
   most of it at a fraction of the 2,390 LUTs; this page's numbers are its
   target, and it should be built against the IM core, not the old one.
5. **FP-RISC's compiler regression** should be looked at separately: both
   classifier images take 1.45x the cycles of the 30 October frozen image,
   and today's `fprisc` head cannot build rv32 bare-metal images at all.
